| ChEBI              | Enriches active ingredients in HealDB with chemical descriptors such as molecular formula, mass, monoisotopic mass, SMILES, InChI, and InChIKey through semantic integration with the ChEBI ontology using SPARQL. | Researchers and professionals working with the chemical characterization of active ingredients, such as chemists, biochemists, and biomedical database developers.                                | ChEBI (Chemical Entities of Biological Interest) is a freely available chemical database and ontology of molecular entities focused on 'small' chemical compounds. It is part of the Open Biomedical Ontologies (OBO) initiative at the European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI).                                                          |
| PubChem            | Retrieves up to three PubMed scientific publications per active ingredient from PubChem CIDs, focusing on bibliographic enrichment using the Entrez API.                                                           | Academic researchers and biomedical professionals interested in identifying recent studies, research trends, and clinical associations related to active ingredients.                             | PubChem is an open chemistry database maintained by the National Institutes of Health (NIH). It serves as a free and publicly accessible resource containing chemical structures, identifiers, physical and chemical properties, biological activities, patents, safety and toxicity data, among other information.                       |
| IUCN               | Integrates data from the IUCN Red List to identify threatened medicinal plants used as active ingredients, including geographic distribution, threats, and conservation status.                                    | Professionals involved in sustainability, environmental conservation and biodiversity research, as well as pharmacists concerned with the environmental impact of plant-based active ingredients. | Established in 1964, the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species is the world’s most comprehensive source of information on the global extinction risk of plants, fungi, and animals. It provides insight into population trends, geographic range, habitat, threats, and conservation actions.                                               |
| RxNorm             | Links HealDB active ingredients to RxNorm using RxCUIs to retrieve standardized names, synonyms, clinical drug presentations, branded formulations, and concept status.                                            | Healthcare systems developers, pharmacists, and clinical data analysts working on drug standardization, interoperability, and integration of prescription data.                                   | RxNorm is both a standardized naming system and a tool designed to support semantic interoperability between drug vocabularies and pharmacy knowledge systems. Developed by the U.S. National Library of Medicine (NLM), RxNorm provides normalized names and unique identifiers (RxCUIs) for medications available in the United States. |
| ATC                | Uses the ATC ontology to classify active ingredients by therapeutic class and compare with classifications in HealDB, enabling alignment and semantic enrichment.                                                  | Researchers and professionals interested in therapeutic classification of medications, drug ontology alignment, and semantic validation of drug datasets.                                         | The Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) Classification System is a drug classification system that classifies the active ingredients of drugs according to the organ or system on which they act and their therapeutic, pharmacological and chemical properties.                                                                        |
| ClinicalTrials.gov | Integrates HealDB with ClinicalTrials.gov to link active ingredients with clinical trials, extracting conditions, interventions, and outcomes using the v2 API.                                                    | Clinical researchers, pharmaceutical product developers, and healthcare professionals tracking research involving specific active ingredients in registered clinical trials.                      | ClinicalTrials.gov is an extensive database of privately and publicly funded clinical studies conducted around the world. It is maintained by the U.S. National Library of Medicine (NLM) and provides detailed information on study design, purpose, eligibility criteria, location, and contact information.                            |
